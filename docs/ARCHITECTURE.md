# Architecture

Econ-Engine separates economic rules, agent strategies, state, events, observer analytics, experiments, and React presentation so causal boundaries remain inspectable.

## MVP8.1 observer architecture

React conditionally mounts Overview, Markets, Households & Labor, Government, and Research. Persistent controls own execution and draft initialization values. Tabs, selected industry, settings disclosure, household query, and deterministic sort order are local UI state only; none enters `stepSimulation` or an RNG source. Hidden charts and large observers are unmounted rather than visually concealed.

Overview provides economy-wide state; domain tabs provide detailed live state; Research hosts explicit independent experiment harnesses. Markets calls legacy `preTaxProfit` operating earnings, while payroll tables call `residualProfit` residual profit. Tables select agents semantically in React and never hide rows by CSS position.

## Simulation core

`types.ts` defines household, firm, Government, event, and bounded daily metric state. `engine.ts` performs immutable daily steps: labor-derived production, spatial consumer markets and Transport payments, inventory expiration, firm pricing decisions, cash-constrained contractual payroll, and finally Government tax/transfer policy. `invariants.ts` validates entity structure and exact stock/flow accounting after each completed day.

`SimulationConfig.householdCount` is the population authority. The canonical MVP8 economy has `N=100` households, while research configurations use complete ten-household blocks, including `N=10`. It has five industries and nine firms: two each in Food, Utilities, Healthcare, and Entertainment, plus one Transport firm. `SimulationConfig.firmsPerIndustry` (default 2) sets how many firms compete in each consumer industry; other values exist to test market structure. Firm IDs follow one scheme, `firm-<industry>-<slot letter>`, built and parsed only by `consumerFirmId` and `firmSlot` in `config.ts`, so no engine or UI logic depends on there being exactly two firms or on particular ID suffixes. The research harnesses that compare Entertainment Firm A with Firm B (the starting-price grid, the spatial competition probe and the generalized spatial analysis) build their own canonical two-firm economies and remain pairwise by design. Each non-Transport consumer firm employs `N/10` workers and Transport employs `N/5`; canonically those counts are 10 and 20. Consumer output is employee count times `laborProductivityUnitsPerWorker`; at the default productivity of five, each consumer firm produces 50 units per day at `N=100`. Transport does not use consumer-production units. Households choose available suppliers by delivered cost under percentage expenditure budgets.

## Daily step

`stepSimulation` copies the previous state (structurally sharing history) and runs named phases in a fixed causal order:

1. `startDay` clears every daily field and records `DAY_STARTED`.
2. `produce` sets each consumer firm's output from its workers' productivity.
3. `runMarkets` opens each consumer industry in turn. For each one, `clearMarket` executes the purchases and `decidePrices` expires unsold stock, books revenue, and runs the pricing strategy for tomorrow.
4. `runPayroll` pays contractual wages from revenue (pro rata when short) and then the 100% corporate profit tax.
5. `runFiscalPhase` lets Government trial a wealth-tax rate, collects the tax, redistributes by water filling, and judges the trial.
6. `buildDayMetrics` and `closeDay` record the bounded metrics, check every invariant, and close the event ledger.

The market RNG is drawn in this exact sequence: each industry's shuffle, then two draws per household for its preferred firm and tie-break number, then one draw per successful purchase, then that industry's price-probe draws, before the next industry opens. Pricing decisions therefore stay inside the per-industry loop rather than running as a separate phase after all markets.

## Market clearing order

Households do not simply buy in random order. For each consumer industry, `clearMarket` builds the purchasing order as follows.

1. **Seeded shuffle.** All households are shuffled with the market RNG.
2. **Preferred firm.** In shuffled order, each household ranks the industry's firms by delivered cost (posted price plus round-trip transport fee) and prefers the cheapest. An exact tie is broken by a seeded draw over the tied firms. Each household then draws a seeded tie-break number, so the RNG consumes two draws per household in shuffled order.
3. **Proximity priority.** At each firm, in firm-ID order, the households that prefer it are queued nearest first (ties by the tie-break number). The first households up to the firm's available stock form the primary queue.
4. **Fallback.** Households beyond a firm's stock form the overflow. The overflow from every firm is ordered by one-way distance to that household's next-best alternative, the cheapest firm by delivered cost other than its preferred one, with ties by the tie-break number. With two firms the alternative is simply the other firm.
5. **Purchase.** The primary queue buys first, then the overflow. Each household buys from the cheapest affordable firm that still has stock (within both its category budget and its cash), choosing among exact ties with a seeded draw. That draw happens for every successful purchase, even when only one firm is cheapest, so the market stream advances once per purchase. A household that can afford no firm records an affordability failure, classified as `category_budget` when even the cheapest delivered cost exceeds its budget and `cash` otherwise. A household that can afford a firm but finds none with stock records a stockout.

**Why.** The MVP4 lab notes record the intent: the nearest households receive earlier inventory access, seeded ties avoid giving priority by household ID, and an affordable fallback is tried before a purchase fails. They do not record why the overflow is ordered by distance to the alternative; in effect it gives the households closest to their second choice the first chance at it. Every step is explicit and seeded from the market stream (rules 7, 8 and 21), so the order is exactly reproducible.

## Strategy boundaries and RNG

`pricingStrategy.ts` owns private firm learning. Firms receive their own realized operating outcomes and, when they run a competitor-anchored experiment, the lowest price a rival in their industry advertised that morning; they do not receive rival sales or profit, household wealth, Gini, Government references, or future information.

`government.ts` owns the bounded Government learner and fiscal rules. Government sees current administered post-payroll cash and its realized policy history. It cannot inspect future markets or simulate counterfactual futures. Market ordering, geography, employment, payroll remainder, firm probing, and Government policy use deterministic seeds; Government has a dedicated substream so its experiments do not perturb unrelated stochastic sequences. Its seed is the salted master seed passed through the nonlinear `mixSeed` bijection, so it is neither equal to nor a fixed XOR offset of the market, spatial, or employment seeds under the linear xorshift generator.

## Fiscal accounting

The authoritative tax rate is integer basis points. Contractual payroll is cash-constrained, so firms can leave wages unpaid. After payroll, each firm's residual profit is explicitly transferred to Government as the fixed 100% corporate-profit tax. Independently, wealth-tax liabilities floor to integer cents and are explicit Household → Government transfers. Deterministic water filling returns the combined Government pool through Government → Household transfers; seeded tied-group ordering allocates indivisible remainder cents. After a completed day, firms and Government hold zero cash, while households collectively hold the full population-derived supply: `householdCount × 5,000 cents` (500,000 cents at canonical `N=100`).

Household state distinguishes pre-tax cash, gross tax, gross transfer, net fiscal transfer, post-fiscal cash, and cumulative fiscal positions. Government state retains incumbent/applied rates, current reference, experiment category/outcome, receipts, transfers, and pre/post Gini.

## Observer analytics and experiments

Live event and metric histories are bounded. `employmentDynamics.ts` preserves the MVP5 007.1 complete finite trajectory analysis. `governmentExperiment.ts` collects compact complete observations over an explicit horizon and compares adaptive Government with an inactive same-seed baseline. It reports policy occupancy/spells, pre/post inequality and concentration, consumption failures, sell-through, revenue, and wages. Observer computations never enter household, firm, or Government decisions.

`ensemble.ts` runs a harness across an explicit seed set and summarizes the spread of its decision-relevant outcomes (mean, sample standard deviation, interpolated 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles, and range). Each seed builds its own economies, so an ensemble never reads or advances a live simulation or its RNG. Results are keyed by seed and reported in ascending seed order, so neither the supplied order nor the order in which parallel workers finish can change a value. `ensembleSeeds(base, n)` starts with the base seed and derives the rest through the Murmur3 finalizer, so adjacent seeds do not feed nearly identical states to the linear xorshift market generator. In the browser, a small pool of Web Workers (one fewer than the CPU cores, at most four) runs seeds in parallel; the pure `runEnsemble` runs them sequentially for tests and scripts.

## Interface boundary

React controls configuration and time and renders Government, household fiscal positions, markets, trajectories, and experiment reports. Horizontal table scrolling preserves compact mobile layouts. No economic rule exists in React.
## Retained MVP7 settlement boundary

After markets and the unchanged price-learning evaluation, firms pay fixed cash-constrained contractual payroll. Residual cash is explicit profit and is transferred to Government as fixed 100% corporate profit tax. Government then collects its independently adaptive household wealth tax and redistributes the combined balance once. Monetary amounts use integer cents; payroll remainder ordering is derived independently from stable seed/day/entity identities.
## MVP8 configurable population

`SimulationConfig.householdCount` is the population authority. Canonical MVP8 uses 100; the scale harness also uses 10. Initial money, household generation, spatial entities, employment slots, production, payroll, demand denominators, and invariants derive from that value. Employment permits complete blocks of one worker per consumer firm plus two for Transport (ten households in the canonical economy), assigning `N/10` workers to every consumer firm and `N/5` to Transport through its isolated subseed. `createSimulation` validates the population, grid and market structure before building any agent.

## MVP9 plots and town layout

`spatial.ts` owns land. Households stand on single tiles (`Household.coordinate`); every firm and Government stands on a `Plot`, a rectangle of tiles whose size comes from `PLOT_SIZES` and `GOVERNMENT_PLOT_SIZE` in `config.ts`. `generateTownLayout` builds the whole town from the spatial seed, which no other stream shares:

1. Government's plot is centred.
2. Firm plots go in descending area, then by ID. A plot that is not square draws its orientation first, then draws its position uniformly from every position that keeps a one-tile gap from the plots already placed. If the drawn orientation fits nowhere, the other is tried.
3. Households are shuffled onto the tiles no plot covers.

The draw sequence is therefore: one orientation draw per non-square plot, one position draw per firm plot, then one draw per household.

`plotDistance` measures from a household's tile to the nearest tile of a plot, and `transportQuote` prices the round trip from it. The engine's delivered-cost ranking, the fallback order and affordability use `transportQuote(household.coordinate, firm.plot, rate)`, as do the observer's territory map and 2D market map, so the display cannot disagree with the model. `validatePopulationConfig` refuses a grid without room for every plot, its gap and one tile per household, and `invariants.ts` checks plot sizes, bounds and gaps and that no household stands on a plot.

The 3D world draws each firm and Government across its plot: `buildingScale` stretches the building model to the plot, less a 0.1-tile inset, and raises it with the square root of the plot's area. The camera's default distance, zoom-out limit, fog and far plane scale with the grid, and Locate stays further out for bigger plots.

## Life: the bottom-up core

`src/life/` is a second simulation core, independent of `src/sim/`. From Life 5 it is frozen as a reference, and new work goes into the valley core below. It imports only the random-number functions from `src/sim/rng.ts`; nothing under `src/sim/` depends on it, so MVP9 is unchanged. The [Life 0](LIFE0_SPEC.md), [Life 1](LIFE1_SPEC.md), [Life 2](LIFE2_SPEC.md), [Life 3](LIFE3_SPEC.md) and [Life 4](LIFE4_SPEC.md) specifications are authoritative; Life 1 generalizes Life 0's single forager to a band, Life 2 gives the band a camp, windfalls, carried food that spoils, and giving, Life 3 adds wood against cold nights, skills that differ, and swapping wood for food, and Life 4 makes skill grow with practice.

- `types.ts` and `config.ts` define the configuration, its defaults and validation. Every food and energy quantity is an integer number of kilocalories. The defaults are the Life 4 world; `LIFE3_WORLD` overrides them back to Life 3's (born skills from 50% to 150%, no practice), `LIFE2_WORLD` builds on it to give back Life 2's (no groves, warm nights, equal skills, no swapping, the mixed band of 12), `LIFE1_WORLD` builds on it to give back Life 1's (no camp, steady harvests, only keepers) and `LIFE0_WORLD` on that to give back Life 0's. Wood is counted in whole units.
- `world.ts` draws patches and, without a camp, every forager's starting tile from a layout seed; with a camp the band starts there and nothing is drawn. Each hour's turn order comes from a turn-order seed derived from the run seed and the hour, and each forager's luck in an hour of foraging from a luck seed derived from the run seed, the hour and the forager, so neither depends on earlier draws. All are derived with `mixSeed`. It also provides the camp tile, strategies by ID, daylight, distance, the x-then-y walking path, and the two timing rules of a camp: when a forager must head home, and which patches it can reach, forage for an hour and walk home from before dusk.
- `food.ts` handles carried food as lots of whole kilocalories with the tick they were harvested: spoiling, taking the oldest first and adding lots in order. `sharing.ts` holds requests (what a hungry forager asks for), the three strategies' order of service, and the ledgers each forager keeps of what it has given and received.
- `forager.ts` holds a forager's side: observation of the patches within its perception radius, hunger with hysteresis, the priority rules (going home first with a camp), patch choice among patches it can reach in time, and choosing again when it sees its target holding less than a full harvest or can no longer reach it. It reads only the forager's state, its memory and the patch underfoot.
- `engine.ts` runs one hour as a pure function, `stepLife(previous) → next`: regrowth, then each living forager's turn (spoilage, eating from what it carries, observation, decision, action, giving, metabolism) in the hour's shuffled order, then the record. Turns see what earlier turns changed, including gifts: a gift made in one turn is eaten at the start of the recipient's next. `lastEvents` holds the hour's actions in turn order and `lastGifts` its gifts; `stockAtTurn` recovers a patch's stock at the start of any turn from them. Events are kept one array per day for the last 30 days; daily summaries, with every forager's end-of-day reserve, are kept for the whole run.
- `invariants.ts` checks exact energy conservation across the band, counting carried and spoiled food, and the other invariants, including one turn per living forager, memories that match what each forager saw at the start of its turn, luck that matches its stream, every gift against its rules and its giver's strategy (replaying the reciprocators' ledgers through the hour), ledgers that agree on both sides, and the whole band at camp every night. `theory.ts` derives the analytical predictions, including the carrying and search capacities and the risk of windfalls. `experiments.ts` runs Life 0's, Life 1's and Life 2's experiments headless, checking invariants every hour. Runs are cached by configuration, since they are deterministic, and end early once everyone is dead.

With 40 foragers, checking every invariant every hour costs about twice the step. Experiments 2 to 4 of Life 1 are tests in their own files (`capacity.test.ts`, `search.test.ts`), and so are Life 2's ensembles (`windfall.test.ts`, `sharing.test.ts`, `spoilage.test.ts`), so vitest runs them in parallel. A canonical Life 2 run of 400 days takes about half a second; the Life tests take about a minute and a half, and use 16 seeds where the experiments' reported numbers use 32.

`src/lifeView/` is the observer, a separate page (`life.html`, a second Vite entry). One `requestAnimationFrame` loop advances a display clock and draws; React only refreshes the HUD ten times a second. The display keeps the states before and after the hour in progress and draws the world part of the way between them (`lifeViewModel.ts`): the forager follows its simulated path at walking speed, berries, trees and the reserve change linearly, and light follows a continuous time of day. It never draws a state the simulation did not produce. A long gap between frames counts as at most a quarter of a second, so a background tab resumes rather than leaping ahead. `lifeScene.ts` owns the three.js scene, with one figure per forager; a band of up to 100 needs no instancing.

The observer follows one forager, which the HUD, the inspector and the reserve chart describe. Foragers sharing a tile stand around a ring; `standingOffsets` places them from a state's positions alone, so the view moves each forager between its places in the two real states it is drawing between. The followed forager's perception is drawn as the staircase outline of the tiles within its radius.

From Life 2, each forager wears its strategy's colour and the followed forager an accent ring at its feet. A bundle beside a forager grows with what it carries, interpolated through the hour. The camp is a ring of stones around a fire that glows as the light fades. A gift in the hour being drawn is an arc from giver to recipient with a parcel travelling along it, anchored on the tile where the gift was made (`giftEnds`), so a giver walking home is not drawn handing food across the board.

From Life 3, `world.ts` also places groves on the tiles after the patches in the layout shuffle, so adding them moves nothing, and draws every forager's foraging and wood skills from a skill stream of their own. A forager's harvest is the larger of `harvestKcal` and its own rate. `wood.ts` holds wood need with hysteresis, the wood target, spare and wanted wood, and an hour of sleep, warm with a fire's worth of wood or cold without. `trade.ts` holds a forager's own terms (its foraging rate over its wood rate), the terms of a pair, whether selling or buying pays at terms it remembers, whom a forager trades with and in what order, and the swap itself, with quotes. All comparisons of terms cross-multiply whole numbers, so they are exact. `forager.ts` chooses the good a forager works for, including trips to gather wood for trade, and applies Life 2's rules to patches or groves; choosing a patch and choosing a grove share one function. A turn gains two phases: exchange (swaps, then Life 2's gifts) and eating again, in which a seller eats the food it received. Swap and quote events go to `lastSwaps` and `lastQuotes`, and each forager keeps running totals of what it has swapped and of its hours foraging and gathering, which the observer reads. A canonical Life 3 run of 400 days takes under a second; the Life tests take about two minutes.

From Life 4, `world.ts` draws born skills rather than rates, and `practice.ts` turns them into rates: the practice percentage from a task's hours over the last `practiceDays` days, rates as the base rate times born skill times practice (practice is 100% when it is off, which gives Life 3's rates exactly), the starting log, and the midnight update. `stepLife` gains a first phase: in the first hour of every day after the first, each living forager enters yesterday's hours in its log, drops the oldest day and takes its rates for the day. Rates are fixed within a day, so every check of an hour's work uses one rate. Each forager counts its hours today and every hour it has entered in its log, and the invariants check that these add up to its hours worked, that its rates are the ones its born skills and log give, and that practice happened only at midnight, once for each forager alive. Practice events go to `lastPractice` and open each day's events; daily summaries carry every forager's rates, which the observer's chart of own terms reads. Life 4's ensembles are tests in their own files (`practice.test.ts`, `division.test.ts`, `memory.test.ts`, `born.test.ts`). A canonical Life 4 run of 400 days, checking every invariant every hour, takes under half a second; the Life tests take about two minutes.

The observer draws each patch as a thicket of six bushes whose berries are its food, and each grove as a stand of five trees: a site is many plants, and fruit marks food while trunks mark wood. `sitePlants` lays a site out within its tile from its kind and ID alone, so it looks the same in every run and draws nothing from the simulation's streams. `fillInTurn` shares a stock out part by part, so berries are picked and trees felled one at a time, the outer bushes first, and the parts drawn stay proportional to what the site holds. A felled tree is a stump that grows back as its wood regrows. All leaves, berries, trunks and crowns are four instanced meshes, and a click on an instance is traced back to its site. Foragers are drawn at half their earlier size, about half as tall as a full-grown tree. It also draws a stack of logs beside each forager holding wood, and each sleeper's own hearth, lit while it burns wood and dark when it sleeps cold. A swap is an arc between seller and buyer on the swap's tile, with a log travelling one way and a parcel the other. Foragers are coloured on a log scale of their own terms, from cyan for those relatively best at gathering to gold for those relatively best at foraging; from Life 4 the scale spans the range born skill and practice allow (`ownTermsRange`), so a band born equal starts one colour and drifts apart. `SpecializationChart.tsx` plots each forager's share of working hours spent gathering against its own terms. The inspector shows a grove as it shows a patch. From Life 4 the HUD shows each practice percentage with the hours behind it and each rate's change at the last midnight, and a chart plots the band's highest and lowest own terms each day with the followed forager's (`ownTermsByDay`).

## Life 5: the valley core

`src/valley/` is the Life core at real scale, from [Life 5](LIFE5_SPEC.md); the hourly core in `src/life/` is frozen as a reference. It imports the random-number functions from `src/sim/rng.ts` and carried-food lots from `src/life/food.ts`, and its experiments import `src/life` to run Life 4 beside it. Nothing in `src/sim/` or `src/life/` depends on it.

- `types.ts` and `config.ts` define the configuration, its defaults and validation. Lengths are whole centimetres, food whole kilocalories and wood whole kilograms; rates and costs stay per hour. `LIFE3_BAND` gives Life 3's band born unequal without practice.
- `layout.ts` draws site centres, each thicket's bushes and each stand's trees from a layout stream, keeps them apart by rejection, and places hearths on a square spiral round the camp. No trigonometry is used anywhere in the core, and distances are exact integer square roots, so runs are identical on every machine. Plants of each kind are kept in typed arrays and bucketed in a grid of cells as wide as a forager's sight, which answers "every plant within this radius" and "the nearest plant that passes this test", searching outwards ring by ring. It also derives the skill and turn-order streams; the turn order is a seeded shuffle per minute.
- `geometry.ts` has integer distance, rounded down and up, walking time in whole seconds, and straight-line steps that never overshoot.
- `forager.ts` holds a forager's side: observation of every plant in view (lists of plants in view are cached by position, holding no simulation state), hunger and the rules in priority order, Life 3's choice of work with loads added, and the choice of a plant: its target unless just seen empty or out of reach in time, else the nearest remembered as holding a minute's work, else the one seen longest ago.
- `work.ts` has the hourly shares of daily regrowth, work and cost paid second by second through remainders in 3,600ths, and the fire decided in the first minute of each hour of sleep.
- `trade.ts` is Life 3's wood need, own terms, swaps and quotes, with talking distance in place of a shared tile, and `practice.ts` is Life 4's practice in seconds.
- `engine.ts` runs one minute: practice at midnight, regrowth on the hour, the turn order, and each living forager's turn (spoilage, eating, observation, decision, the minute's 60 seconds, exchange, eating again, metabolism), then the record. A minute may hold a walk and then work. `stepValley(previous)` copies the state and advances the copy; `runValley(state, minutes)` copies once and advances its copy minute by minute, calling back after each minute, and can skip keeping the event history, which experiments do. Both leave the state they are given unchanged, and `n` steps equal one run of `n` minutes. The layout and finished days are shared between states; plant stocks and forager memories are typed arrays copied with the state. Carried food is eaten and added without copying or sorting lots needlessly, with exactly the result of Life's `takeOldest` and `addLots`.
- `invariants.ts` is a checker that keeps its own copy of every plant's stock and replays each minute's regrowth and recorded work onto it. It checks exact energy and wood conservation every minute; the plants worked against the replay every minute and every plant every hour; seconds, movement, reach, work in daylight, memory of the plant worked or targeted every minute and of everything in view every hour; sleep at the hearth, fires, loads, carried lots, terms heard, practice, rates and swaps.
- `theory.ts` predicts time use with walking, the valley's capacity, and the largest load that can never leave a forager with spare wood. `experiments.ts` runs Life 5's five experiments headless with the checker on, caching runs by configuration, and summarizes them as Life 4's experiments did, beside rows from the hourly core.

A canonical run of 400 days takes about 2 seconds, and about 3 with the checker. The experiments are tests in their own files so they run in parallel; they use 8 seeds, and shorter horizons where an expectation holds at any horizon. The test runner's limit per test is 60 seconds, because the experiments load every core.

`src/valleyView/` is the observer on `life.html`; the hourly observer in `src/lifeView/` moved, unchanged, to `life4.html`. As before, one `requestAnimationFrame` loop advances a display clock and draws, React refreshes the HUD ten times a second, and the display draws between two real states, now a minute apart (`valleyViewModel.ts`). A forager walks the walking seconds of its minute along a straight line and then works or stands; its gesture (walk, pick, gather, stand, sleep) follows its recorded action, and only the gesture's loop is for the eye. When several minutes run in one frame, all but the last run in one go without keeping events. `valleyScene.ts` owns the three.js scene in metres: bushes as three leafy lobes with berries, trees as a trunk and two cones with a pile of deadwood at the foot, all instanced, with only plants whose stock changed redrawn; people about 1.75 m tall built from capsules that swing at hips and shoulders and bend at the waist; a bundle on the back for the wood of the trip under way and a stack by the hearth for the rest; each forager's hearth, lit in its warm hours; and the camp's fire. The camera is a strategy-game camera: a point on the ground, a distance, a turn and a pitch, panned by dragging or the arrow and WASD keys, zoomed by wheel or pinch, turned by right-drag, two fingers or Q and E, tilting towards eye level close in, and easing after the followed forager until the viewer pans. Shadows cover the part of the valley in view, so they stay sharp at any zoom. The colour scale and specialization chart are shared with `src/lifeView`, whose types now accept any configuration with the fields they read.
